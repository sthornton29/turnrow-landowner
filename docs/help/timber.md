## What a stand is

A timber stand is an area managed as one unit: planted pine of one age, a hardwood bottom, a mixed block. Each has a type (planted pine, natural pine, hardwood, mixed, other), species, the year it was planted or established, site index, trees per acre planted, a main goal (timber, wildlife, both, looks, or conservation), notes, and a boundary. The Timber page lists stands by property with each stand's age, last thinning and burn, and its next step.

## Upcoming on your land

The top of the Timber page gathers the work that needs attention across all stands: thinnings that are due, burns, replanting after a harvest. Routine items (walking each stand once a year, a forester walk-through for hardwoods) are grouped into one line each. The schedule comes from typical timing for the stand type:

- **Planted pine**: herbicide release in years 1 to 2, first thinning around age 13 to 18 (earlier on better ground: add the site index for a sharper window), a prescribed burn every 3 to 5 years after thinning (2 to 3 if the goal is wildlife), a second thinning 6 to 10 years after the first, final harvest around age 28 to 38, and replanting after a clearcut.
- **Natural pine, hardwood, mixed**: a forester walk-through about every 10 years, and the burn cycle kept going if burns are on record.
- **Every stand**: walk it once a year and look for fading or red pine crowns (beetles), storm damage, and trespass.

These are starting points for a conversation with your forester, not orders.

## Recording what happened (a stand's history)

On a stand page, **Record work** asks "What happened here?" with plain buttons: Planted, Sprayed, Burned, Thinned, Harvested, Walked it, Storm or bugs, Site prep, Fertilized, Cruised, and more. Then a short form:

- **Already done** or **Planning to do it** (planned work shows at the top of the history until you mark it done).
- **The year** (enough on its own) or an exact date.
- **Which stands**: one burn or spray can cover several stands of the property; it then shows in each stand's history.
- **More details** for the specifics: species, seedlings per acre and genetics for a planting; product and rate for a spray; burn manager and AFC permit number for a burn; buyer and kind of thinning; cause and acres for damage; the forester's value estimate for a cruise.
- **Paperwork**: each history entry has its own documents (invoices, burn permits, cruise reports).

Each suggestion under **Coming up** has three answers: **Already done: record it** (opens the form, already filled in), **Schedule it** (planned work), or **Not needed** (the suggestion goes away; the answer is kept under "marked not needed" in case you change your mind).

## Costs and taxes

Add each bill to the work it paid for (+ Add a cost): amount, date paid, who was paid, and what for. Every cost asks one question, "This cost was for:", and keeps the matching tax category:

- Getting new trees growing (site prep, seedlings, planting, early spraying): **replanting cost**, which counts toward the Section 194 replanting deduction.
- Upkeep of the woods (burning, firebreaks, spraying older trees, advice): **upkeep expense**.
- Selling timber (the forester's sale fee, a cruise for the sale, legal): **selling expense**, which lowers the taxable gain on the sale.
- Something that lasts (a road, culvert, gate): **lasting improvement**.
- Not sure: left for the accountant.

The app suggests the category from the kind of work (a spray in the first three years after planting counts as replanting). The stand page totals recorded costs by category. These are for your accountant: confirm with them before filing.

## Adding stands

- On the Map: + Add, then Timber stand. The save form asks the stand type (required) and prefills Loblolly pine for pine picks.
- From Timber Scan: let the app propose stands from satellite and crop data, then accept the ones that look right (see Timber Scan).
- From a file: shapefile or KML through Import.

## Working with stands

- **Split**: from the map panel, draw a line across a stand to cut it into two with the details copied; useful after a partial harvest.
- **Colors**: planted pine deep teal, natural pine olive, hardwood burnt orange, mixed violet, other gray, with a legend on the map.
- **Sales**: a stand page shows the sales it is part of and the timber income allocated to it. Thinning and harvest entries can link to the sale.
- **Forested wetlands** stay timber stands (hardwood with a note); the Wetland type is for open marsh and sloughs.

## Common questions

- **The last thinning or burn year is wrong.** Record the thinning or burn in the stand's history; the latest one wins. The older "last thinning year" and "last burn year" fields in Edit are for history from before you started recording, and still count.
- **The schedule says a thinning is past due, but it was thinned.** Use "Already done: record it" with the year it happened; the schedule moves on to the next step.
- **Why does it ask for the planting year?** The planted pine schedule is built from the stand's age. Add the year in Edit and the suggestions appear.
- **Two stands should be one.** Edit the boundary of one with Add area to cover the other, then delete the spare.
- **Deleting an entry with paperwork.** Delete or move its documents first, so nothing is orphaned.
